Your biggest enemy isn't the competition. It's your own desire to over-engineer.
If you know how to code, your default instinct is to open VS Code. Stop.

***

Step 1: Find a painful, boring workflow.
Don't build "another to-do app." Build an automation that saves a specific type of professional 5 hours a week.
Boring B2B problems are where the actual money is.

***

Step 2: The No-Code Stack.
You only need three things:
- Carrd or Webflow for the landing page.
- Airtable or Notion for the database.
- Make or Zapier for the backend logic.
That's your entire tech stack.

***

Step 3: The "Wizard of Oz" MVP.
Your app doesn't need to be fully automated yet.
If a user clicks "Generate Report," you can literally receive an email, manually compile the data, and send it back.
They just care about the result, not how the sausage is made.

***

Step 4: The 2-Hour Landing Page.
Copywriting matters more than design.
State the problem. Show the solution. Have a single, clear pricing tier.
If you are spending more than two hours tweaking CSS, you are procrastinating.

***

Step 5: The "Sell Before You Build" strategy.
Put a Stripe checkout link on the page before the product is even finished.
If nobody is willing to put their credit card down for the promise of the solution, they won't pay for the actual product.

***

If you charge $49/month, you only need 20 paying customers to hit $1,000 in monthly recurring revenue.
That is entirely achievable for a solo founder.

***

Use the revenue to justify the engineering time. Code should scale a validated business, not validate an idea.

***

Cancel your weekend plans.
